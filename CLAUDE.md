A CAD application in modern .NET built around a **hybrid geometry kernel** that natively supports three representations:

- **B-Rep** — parametric surfaces (planes, conics, NURBS) wrapped in topology, for precision modeling
- **Implicit** — signed distance fields (SDF) as an AST of primitives and operators, for lattices and organic blends
- **Mesh** — discrete half-edge triangle meshes, for rendering, FEA, and 3D printing

A distinctive design goal is **LINQ-native geometry querying**: a custom `IQueryable` provider inspects expression trees and routes spatial predicates to spatial indexes (BVH/octree) instead of linear scans.

A blanket "the volumes agree" would have passed a model with K wired to a constant. Round joins are **inscribed** arcs, matching `Sketch.ToRegions`' one-sided contract so error never accumulates outward. **Open-path stroking** (`Region2dOffset.Stroke(path, width, cap, join)` — toolpath footprints, slots, SVG strokes) rides the same union: full-width slabs, corner joins offered on BOTH sides (the inner wedge is already inside its slabs, and a 180° reversal legitimately fills both — the round nose on a doubled-back path), butt/round/square caps; self-crossings just union and a closed circuit encloses its hole. `Shape.Section(plane)` = OpenSCAD `projection(cut = true)`, exact from the B-Rep surfaces when the shape lowers (fidelity set by chord tolerance alone) and mesh-derived otherwise — sealed internal cavities come back as holes; a plane flush with a face or containing an edge is refused (that section is an area, not a curve). `Shape.Silhouette(plane)` = `projection(cut = false)`, the union of front-facing projected triangles folded through a **balanced tree over Morton-sorted faces** — measured on a 64-segment torus: **67 ms sorted+tree vs 2.4 s tree-unsorted vs 259 s linear**, because merging face 1 with face 900 gives two disjoint regions and cancels nothing. `Shape.Wedge` (OCCT `BRepPrimAPI_MakeWedge`) **is an extrusion** — a trapezoidal XZ section swept along y — so it carries one internally and every lowering delegates: no fourth code path, native in all three reps, exact under any affine. Four numerical lessons, each of which cost real geometry: **(a)** a miter apex must divide by `sum.LengthSquared`, never `sum.Length` squared — at a right angle those are exactly 2 and 2.0000000000000004, which tilts the apex a few ULPs off both offset lines so the collinear T-junctions stop collapsing and a mitered square returns with 8 corners; **(b)** `Arrangement2d` hole assignment must be by **connected component** (union-find over edges), not by area+containment — a lone convex cell adopted its OWN reversed perimeter as a hole (the two shoelace sums differ by an ULP and every vertex is shared, so the containment probe sat exactly ON the boundary and decided by luck), cancelling the cell to ~1e-16 and silently vanishing a whole operand from a union; loops of one component can never nest, which is the structural fix; **(c)** `FaceGeometry.Contains`' one-sided upward-v ray **cannot see a rim below the probe**, so it calls every point on a pole-bounded face outside (a sphere's cross-section came back empty) — run the parity in BOTH v directions: they agree on a properly closed trim and disagree exactly when one side is a pole; **(d)** independently-evaluated mesh vertices projected to 2D are only ULP-equal, so two edges that should be collinear sit ~2e-16 apart — too small for the arrangement to see as a T-junction, too large to ignore — and the sliver's interior sample rounds onto its own boundary, making bulk unions **order-dependent** (60.42 vs 59.33 on a 16-segment torus; a 64-segment one threw outright). `PlanarSection.SilhouetteGrid` quantizes projected coordinates at 1e-12 of the outline extent (scale-free tier) and every merge order then agrees. **Exact curved 2D booleans** (`CurvedEdge2d`/`CurvedRegion2d`/`CurveIntersection2d`/`CurvedArrangement2d`/`CurvedRegion2dBoolean`/`CurvedRegion2dOffset` in Core.Geometry2; `Curve2d.TryToCurvedEdge`/`FromCurvedEdge` and `Profile.FromCurvedRegion` in BRep; `Sketch.ToCurvedRegions`/`FromCurvedRegion`/`UnionExact`/`IntersectExact`/`SubtractExact`/`OffsetExact` in Modeling): everything built from a `Region2d` used to inherit chord flattening — offsets, sketch booleans, sections, silhouettes, and the `Profile`s an extrusion is built from — because arcs were polygonized before the arrangement saw them. The curved tier carries **lines and circular arcs through the arrangement unflattened**: a disc measures πr², two overlapping discs' union/intersection/difference match the analytic lens formulas to 1e-10, round offset joins are **exact sectors** (which RETIRES the inscribed-arc contract rather than honouring it — an exactly-offset arc IS the true offset), and a 40×20 plate with a Ø12 bore extruded 5 mm comes out within 1e-6 relative of (800 − 36π)·5 where the default-flattened route is 3.6e-5 off — an error that is a FLOOR no tessellation density can lower, since it is baked into the profile before any solid exists. **A PARALLEL type, not an extension of `Arrangement2d`**: the straight arrangement is boolean-critical and pinned bit for bit (now by `Region2dGoldenTests`' committed fingerprints as well as by the rendered PNGs), and teaching it curves would change its fan comparator (positions → tangents), its edge identity (a vertex pair → a vertex pair PLUS a carrier, since two points on one circle are joined by two arcs and by a chord) and its area rule at once, inside the code with the widest regression surface — the same call design.md §5 makes for `FaceSplitter`. **Lines and arcs is a COMPLETE tier, not a budget**: the cell walk orders edges at a node by departure TANGENT with departure CURVATURE as the tie-break (from p(s) = v + s·d + ½s²κ·n̂), and for lines and circles agreeing in both means sharing a carrier — a line and a circle never osculate, and two circles that osculate are one circle — so the tie-break is complete and the walk never guesses, whereas two Béziers can agree to second order and separate only in the third derivative. Béziers therefore stay flattened at the entry points (stated in the API contract, not hidden) and the comparator refuses by name if ever handed a second-order tie between different carriers. **ONE tolerance, and it is a LENGTH** — the arrangement's own vertex snap distance, with no second epsilon in the tier: a line is tangent to a circle when the centre's distance from it differs from the radius by less than it, two circles when their centre distance differs from r₀+r₁ or |r₀−r₁| by less than it, a point is on an edge when its distance is under it. **Near-tangency SNAPS to one touch point rather than refusing or dropping the contact**: both alternatives are unstable (two crossings a nanometre apart is a sliver cell classified by rounding), snapping is area-neutral to O(τ^1.5), and a tangential contact is *representable* here precisely because the two edges leave the node with equal tangents and different curvature. The interior sample gains one thing the straight proof did not need — the push is capped by the edge's own **curvature radius**, or a small circular hole inside a large cell sends the sample past the circle's centre and out the far side — and classification uses the epsilon-free `ParityInside` rather than the closed-set `Contains`, whose on-boundary band would answer "inside both" for a cell thinner than the weld tolerance. Section loop assembly **never welds**: crossings are solved once per EDGE on that edge's exact curve so both adjacent faces get the same point, and endpoints are node positions rather than the curve re-evaluated at a searched parameter (a ternary search left ~5e-11, enough to put a box corner at x = −4.999999999963106). **Debug modifiers + validation report**: `Part.Ghost/Hidden/Isolated` (OpenSCAD %/*/! analog; `#` highlight stays the selection mechanism) with ALL rules in `DebugFilter` (Modeling) — Ghost renders translucent via `Part.EffectiveDisplayMode` (the property every render path reads: window `EffectiveModeOf`, offscreen, web frame build) but never exports; Hidden neither renders nor exports (byte-identical offscreen render to a scene never containing it — it must not even influence framing); Isolated shows/exports only isolated parts — shared by SceneHost visibility, `RenderToImage`, `--export` (all formats incl. ## Architecture

Three engines with different mathematics and data structures, plus interop and query layers on top of a shared core.

### Core (foundation for everything)
- Zero-allocation math: `readonly struct` `Vector3d`, `Matrix4x4d`, quaternions, `AABB`
- Central tolerance/epsilon policy for robust floating-point comparison
- Spatial acceleration: BVH and octree (used by all engines and by the query layer)
- Central **unit** policy: `ModelUnits` (mm/N/MPa/tonne/s, densities in tonne/mm³, conversions at the edges) and the `Material`/`Materials` both the document model and `EngrCAD.Fea` share — the only assembly both see

### Mesh engine (discrete)
- Half-edge data structure for O(1) topology traversal
- Algorithms: booleans, decimation, subdivision
- Bulk data stored data-oriented (SoA) for cache locality

### Implicit engine (volumetric)
- SDF evaluator: `(x, y, z) → distance`
- Primitives (sphere, box, cylinder, …) and operators (union, intersect, smooth blend) composed as an AST
- SIMD-batched evaluation; later, compilation of C# expression trees down to SDF graphs / IL / compute shaders

### B-Rep engine (parametric) — hardest, built last
- Geometry: planes, cylinders, cones, NURBS surfaces/curves
- Topology wrapper referencing geometry: Solid → Shell → Face → Loop → Edge → Vertex
- Surface–surface intersection engine; booleans and filleting on top

### Interop layer — conversion triangle complete
- Implicit → Mesh: `SurfaceNets.Polygonize` (manifold dual contouring)
- B-Rep → Mesh: `BRepTessellator.Tessellate` (shared edge polylines + ear clipping + welding)
- Mesh → Implicit: `MeshSdf` (BVH nearest-triangle + angle-weighted pseudonormal sign; requires closed mesh; composes with all `Sdf` operators)
- Field → Mesh quality: `SdfProjectionTarget` implements EngrCAD.Mesh's `IProjectionTarget` over any `Sdf` (`p − d·∇d/|∇d|`, iterated; difference step relative to the field's bounds). **The guarantee is one-sidedness, not convergence**: a 1-Lipschitz lower bound puts the surface at least `|d|` away in every direction, so a step of that length can never cross it — but `|d|` need not decrease, and near a CSG difference's **fictitious faces** (the subtracted tool's own surface, inside removed material) two branches trade the point back and forth forever (measured on `Box(2,2,2) − Sphere(1.2)`: six steps leave a probe exactly where it started, true distance 0.45 vs a field reading 0.14). Harmless for the job it exists for — a remeshed vertex is always within a fraction of an edge length of the surface, where every field here is locally exact — so project near-surface points only, never as a general closest-point query

### Simulation layer (`EngrCAD.Fea`) — tet meshing + linear-static structural + thermal + modal + buckling/harmonic done
- Closed surface mesh → `TetMesh`: exact-predicate Delaunay, winding-number classification, verified boundary recovery, optional quality/sizing refinement
- **Anisotropic boundary layers** (`BoundaryLayerSpec` on `TetMeshOptions`): a graded stack marched inward from a `Facets`-selected wall — the same selector a boundary condition uses — with the isotropic pipeline filling what is left, so no new volume algorithm and every existing guarantee inherited
- `QuadraticTetMesh` (10-node), `TetQuality` (dihedral + radius-edge + slivers, partitioned by measured stretch so the isotropic rules are never applied to a deliberately flat layer element), per-element region ids, per-facet source-triangle tags
- **Structural**: `AnalysisMesh` (one view over 4- and 10-node elements) → `StructuralModel` (materials per region, supports and loads over facet selectors) → `StructuralSolver` (AMD-ordered `SparseCholesky` or CG) → `StructuralResults` (displacement, strain, stress, von Mises, `MeshField` publishing, `.vtu`)
- **Thermal**: the same triple with one temperature DOF per node — `ThermalModel` → `ThermalSolver.Solve`/`SolveTransient` → `ThermalResults`/`ThermalTransientResults` — over the same `AnalysisMesh` and the same `Facets` selectors, plus one-way coupling back via `StructuralModel.ThermalLoad`. Shared rather than parallel-built: `FeaGuards` (the element-Jacobian guard both solvers ask), `SurfaceSampler` (the display-mesh correspondence both results types publish through), `StructuralSolver.AdvisoryFor` (the direct-vs-CG note)
- **Modal**: `ModalSolver.Solve` → `ModalResults` (`VibrationMode` frequencies/shapes/participation, `RigidBodyMode` kept separate) over the same `AnalysisMesh` and the same supports — a consistent mass matrix that IS `ThermalElement.Capacity`'s integral under another name (one implementation, `TetElement.ConsistentMass`, and one rule selector `TetQuadrature.ForMass` two degrees above the stiffness's), plus shift-and-invert Lanczos (`LanczosEigen`) over ONE `SparseCholesky` factorization. `RigidBodyModes` is shared with `StructuralSolver`, which REFUSES on the same computation this one REPORTS
- **Buckling / stress stiffening / harmonic**: `BucklingSolver.Solve(staticResults)` → `BucklingResults` over `TetElement.GeometricStiffness` (the mass matrix's shape with `∫grad N_a·σ·grad N_b` in it), `ModalSolveOptions.Prestress` for a preloaded modal solve, and `HarmonicSolver` → `HarmonicResponse` for a steady-state sweep by modal superposition over `RayleighDamping`/`ModalDamping`. `FeaAssembly` is the shared stiffness/geometric assembly so two eigen-solvers cannot build different `K`s
- **`Material`/`Materials` live in `EngrCAD.Core`**, not here: `EngrCAD.Modeling`'s `Part.Material` needs the same type (mass properties, the BOM, the default display colour) and Core is the only assembly both see. Analysis properties are optional (0 = not stated), so the refusals live at the point of use and name the property — `StructuralModel` on the modulus, `ThermalSolver` on conductivity/capacity, `ModalSolver` on density. Units are `ModelUnits`' mm/N/MPa/tonne/s
- **Cancellable**: every solve entry point takes Core's optional trailing `ProgressCancel`, and it is honest because `SparseCholesky.Factorize` honours it (per eliminated column) — adding it here first would have advertised a cancellation that cannot reach 99% of the work. `SparseSymmetricCG.Solve` takes one too, so the promise holds whichever method runs
- Depends on Core + Mesh only; nothing depends on it (a clean leaf) — results leave as `MeshField`s, which is what lets the viewer colour-map them without referencing this project. Next: transient dynamics (Newmark/HHT), block Lanczos for multiplicity ≥ 3, a direct per-frequency harmonic solve

### Query layer (LINQ) — v1 implemented
- `SpatialCollection<T>` (items + bounds expression + BVH) with `AsQueryable()`: a custom `IQueryProvider` rewrites expression trees, recognizes `SpatialPredicates` clauses (`.Within(box)`, `.WithinDistance(p, d)`, `.HitBy(ray)`) applied to the registered bounds accessor, answers them from the BVH, and re-applies the full predicate over candidates (interception is a pure optimization). Residual/non-spatial queries fall back to LINQ-to-Objects. `LastQueryUsedIndex` is the diagnostic.
- IMPORTANT: query predicates use the by-value `SpatialPredicates` extension methods, NOT `Aabb.Intersects` — expression trees cannot contain calls with `in` parameters, which the kernel API uses.
- Topology-traversal LINQ exists on mesh handles (`vertex.OutgoingHalfEdges().Where(...)`, `face.AdjacentFaces()`, `Face.Bounds`).
- Future: metadata indexes for B-Rep feature queries (cylindrical faces by radius), expression-tree→SDF compilation.

## Performance mandates (non-negotiable in kernel code)

- Math types are `readonly struct`; hot paths allocate nothing on the heap
- Use `Span<T>`/`Memory<T>`; temporaries come from `ArrayPool<T>` or `stackalloc`, never `new` per call
- Use `System.Runtime.Intrinsics` (SIMD) for SDF evaluation, ray/primitive intersection, and other batch kernels
- Bulk mesh data uses structs-of-arrays, not arrays-of-objects
- Never compare floats with `==`; all comparisons go through the central tolerance policy in `EngrCAD.Core`
- **A sampling grid in PARAMETER space says nothing about coverage in MODEL space — and the safe way to fix that is to append, never to replace.** The marching tracer seeded both surfaces on a fixed 24×24 (u, v) grid. On an M8 thread band that is ~330 mm long and 0.16 mm tall (aspect ~2000) it puts columns 13 mm apart along the band while spending 24 rows across a strip a sixth of a millimetre wide — so a Ø6 cross-drill's entire window falls between two columns and the intersection returns **zero** branches (the flank band, six). `SurfaceIntersection` now runs a SECOND pass for surfaces past an aspect threshold, re-gridding at the same sample budget with `nu/nv = aspect` so spacing is equal in millimetres; the crest band returns three branches and the flank nineteen. **The isotropic pass still runs first, in its original order**, and `March` skips any seed already covered by a traced branch — so every branch the old grid found is traced from the same seed and comes back bit-identical, and the whole suite (3891 tests) plus every docs render is unchanged. That ordering is the entire safety argument for touching the tracer at all. Two sub-lessons: the extents must be measured as **mean |∂P/∂u| × domain length, never as a chordal polyline** (a chord between two samples of a coiled band measures ACROSS the coil — an 8-sample polyline read 61 mm where the band is 327 mm long), and the speed samples must be taken at cell CENTRES, since `Eval` clamps to the domain and a central difference at a boundary node is silently one-sided and halves what it reports.
- **A nearest-first branch and bound is already its own seed.** Seeding `Bvh.Nearest` with the previous coherent sample's answer (distance is 1-Lipschitz) is provably result-identical given strict `>` pruning and a strictly-greater seed — and buys only **1.12–1.20×** on the most coherent run available, plus a small net *loss* on scattered probes. Descending the nearer child first already reaches a tight bound in O(log n) node tests, so a seed can only save part of the first descent. `MeshSdf` therefore keeps the base class's scalar batch loop, and `MeshSdfBatchTests` pins batch == scalar bit for bit so the next attempt is held to the same bar.
- **Never benchmark an optimization against a baseline you wrote differently.** A prototype of that same seeding measured 1.88×; its baseline went through a `Func` delegate per triangle while the seeded path called the kernel directly. The entire gap was the delegate.
- **A packet query's shared bound is governed by its group's DIAMETER — and the batch seam delivers collinear ROWS, not compact blocks.** The other lever on `MeshSdf`'s 74–85% `Bvh.Nearest` cost was a packet: one traversal per coherent group, per-point pruning at the leaves. Built and measured (`MeshSdfPacketBenchmark`) against the production `Bvh.Nearest` with the same struct metric: a compact 2³ block of grid points wins **1.45×**, the same 8 points laid out as a z-consecutive row **0.86×**, and 64 of them — spanning 1.9 units on a 3-unit model, so the shared bound becomes the whole model and the packet degenerates into a brute-force scan — **0.30×**. A node is visited whenever it could beat the *worst* member's best, so spread kills it. `Sdf.Evaluate(points, distances)` is a flat span every bulk consumer generates z-fastest, and `MeshSdf` cannot regroup a collinear run into blocks; teaching the batch contract to carry "these points form a compact block" is a large API change to buy a win in a shape no caller produces. Tie-breaking never became the question. Two negatives ride along: seeding the packet from one exact query at the group's centre changes nothing and makes the best case *worse* (the nearest-first lesson again), and pruning on **squared** distances throughout — removing a `Math.Sqrt` from every box and triangle test — is 0.94–0.99×.
- **A continuation needs a complete seed set; the only sound way to prove one complete is a cull; and the cull has already produced the visit set, so the continuation adds nothing.** That is why `SurfaceNets` visits a shell around the surface by *culling* rather than by seeding and flooding (`SurfaceCull`). The argument needs one property and no epsilon: an `Sdf` here is 1-Lipschitz, so every point p of a block with centre c and half-diagonal R satisfies |d(p)| ≥ |d(c)| − |c − p| ≥ |d(c)| − R, and |d(c)| > R therefore rules out a sign change anywhere in it. Cell and quad loops keep their exact (j, k) order and only skip runs of tiles, so output is **bit-identical** including face ordering. Two implementation notes: the x/y dilation must be computed per row rather than baked into a stored mask (dilating a block per axis costs a block of extra samples on three sides of the surface — most of what the cull just saved), and the one-cell slack on the radius is cushion for blends, which are only approximately Lipschitz, not part of the argument.
- **An 8× saving in the dominant cost bought 2.5×, because the dominant cost stopped being dominant.** The cull evaluates 28.4% / 15.6% / 12.0% of the grid at resolution 96 / 192 / 256 and buys 1.60× / 2.18× / 2.46× (a `MeshSdf` field, 2.99× at res 96). Measured at res 256 with a field costing one square root per sample, the whole call still takes 132.8 ms against 129.3 ms for the real CSG field: evaluation is now free and the cost is assembly — `HalfEdgeMesh.Build` alone is 39–48%. **Re-measure where the time goes after every win**, or the next optimization goes into the part that no longer matters.
- **The half-edge builder's twin resolution is a COUNTING SORT, and the grid's contribution was the flat buffer, not the adjacency.** Following the finding above into assembly: the cost was a `Dictionary<(int, int), int>` probed twice per directed edge (over a million tuple hashes at resolution 256) plus one `int[4]` heap allocation per quad. Both are gone — every directed edge is filed under `min(from, to)`, so an edge and its reverse share a bucket that holds only one vertex fan (three to six entries), and ONE scan of it finds the non-manifold duplicate and the twin at once; buckets fill in ascending half-edge order, so "already seen" is a `break` and the scan observes exactly what the dictionary probe observed, error-detection order included, which is what keeps every exception message identical. Measured interleaved in one sitting: assembly **3.3–3.7×** (47.5 → 13.4 ms at 129 268 vertices), whole polygonization 1.2–1.5×, allocation at res 256 **145 → 103 MB**, output bit-identical with the golden fingerprints untouched. **Two things worth keeping.** The plan was "build the half-edge structure from the known grid adjacency, keeping the generic `Build` as a debug cross-check" — but the grid does NOT hand over twins for free (a dual edge is a grid FACE, and matching its up-to-four claimants needs an O(n³) face table the streaming window cannot hold), whereas the counting sort is O(n) with no hashing and serves every caller in the repo. Making the generic path fast beat making a second special one, and it left ONE implementation instead of two-plus-a-cross-check. And the honest closing number is the SHARE, not the speedup: assembly went 38–51% → 15–18% of the call, so the builder has stopped being the place to look and the remaining ~175 ms of a 213 ms res-384 polygonize is the grid walk itself.
- **A contract that holds for the inputs you tried is not a contract, and "manifold Surface Nets" was one of those.** `SurfaceNets.Polygonize` is documented as the MANIFOLD variant — one vertex per connected component of a cell's inside corners, the rule that exists precisely to stop thin sheets and saddles going non-manifold — and it still threw `Directed edge 4954 → 4967 appears twice` out of `HalfEdgeMesh.Build` on `Sphere(16).Lattice(Gyroid(12, 1.2))` at resolution 88. **The gap is Marching Cubes' AMBIGUOUS FACE**: a grid face whose inside corners are exactly a *diagonal* pair has all four of its edges crossing, so it carries two quad edges between the same two cells; when BOTH cells join that pair into one component, the two quads land on the same directed edge. That is the ONLY way a duplicate can arise — two instances share a vertex pair, hence a cell pair, hence one face, and a face with just two crossings gives its two instances opposite directions — so the fix is exact rather than a heuristic: test each interior face once, from its + side, and where both cells join, split that component **by the outside blob each crossing reaches**. The split provably always exists, because a cell can never join both an ambiguous face's inside pair AND its outside pair (a path between the inside pair must use an off-diagonal corner of the far face as *inside*, while a path between the outside pair needs both of them *outside*). **Three things worth keeping.** The per-cell map had to be re-keyed from inside CORNER to cube EDGE, since one corner's two crossings can now land on different vertices. **The conservative version of the same fix was 17× larger and moved a golden**: triggering on the cell's own ambiguous face without consulting the neighbour split 204 cells instead of 12 and changed the `torus` fingerprint, where the exact trigger leaves every golden fingerprint and every rendered docs PNG byte-identical — the difference between "fix the broken configuration" and "fix everything that looks like it". And **whether the configuration lands is ALIGNMENT, not tolerance** (the torus-silhouette-pinhole lesson again): the reported lattice fails at resolution 88 and at none of the other nine tried, so the regression test is a family sweep over fields *and* resolutions, with each fixture asserted to still CARRY the configuration so it cannot quietly stop testing it. The finding that settles what the bug was about: a plain `Sphere(10).Shell(0.6)` at resolution 44 reproduces it with 240 such faces. A one-cell-thick wall is the cause; lattices were just where it was noticed.
- **A direct-vs-iterative crossover measured on ONE operator is a measurement of that operator's conditioning, not of the two algorithms.** `SparseCholesky` vs `SparseSymmetricCG` was benchmarked on grid Laplacians **plus an identity shift**, which is strongly diagonally dominant — so CG converged in an *n-independent* ~35 iterations at every size, and the recorded verdict ("past ~14k unknowns one-shot CG wins") was really "CG wins on a well-conditioned system", which is not news. Drop the shift for a pure Dirichlet Laplacian, the regime an FEA stiffness matrix lives in, and at 62 500 unknowns CG needs **858 iterations / 750 ms** against AMD-ordered Cholesky's **221 ms factor + 5.8 ms per solve** — the direct solve wins on the FIRST right-hand side, by 3.3×, the exact opposite conclusion from the same code at the same size. Vary the CONDITIONING as well as the size, or the table describes a fixture. (AMD itself: 4.6–13.4× on factor time, 3.5–8.3× on fill, never a loss — but opt-in, because a fill-reducing permutation changes the summation order and every committed upstream number was measured natural.) **The FEA work then confirmed this rule by contradicting the verdict**: on 3D elasticity over tetrahedra, Jacobi-preconditioned CG beats AMD-ordered Cholesky in three of four cases at a few thousand unknowns (37/153/334 ms against 65/524/342), and the unblocked up-looking factorization takes **79 s** for a 46 800-DOF system against 323 ms to assemble it — a third operator, a third answer. AMD's own leverage grows the other way: **11.3× less fill and 191× faster** (19 383 964 → 1 723 544 entries, 64 834 → 340 ms) on a 6 552-DOF quadratic system, so the ordering is what makes second-order FEA possible at all while the factorization itself is the remaining wall.
- **A progress fraction must count WORK, and whether that differs from counting iterations is a property of the operator rather than of the algorithm.** `SparseCholesky.Factorize` now takes a `ProgressCancel` and reports the numeric pass's exact inner-loop update count — available for free, since the symbolic pass has already counted every column of L and column j is used by exactly as many rows as it has off-diagonal entries, so the total is `Σ c_j(c_j+1)/2` before the first multiply. The interesting half is the measurement that the obvious justification is only sometimes true: on a **dense** factor the halfway column has spent 12.5% of the arithmetic (cubic accumulation), so column number would read 50% and be wrong by four times — but on a **banded** one, which is exactly the natural-ordered 2D grid Laplacian this class was written for, the two agree at 0.468. So the re-weighting is free where it changes nothing and load-bearing where it does, and the regression test uses the dense fixture on purpose while recording the banded number beside it. Two riders: the accumulator sits behind the null check because it is the innermost loop in the library, and reports are throttled to 200 steps because a factorization has as many columns as unknowns and a UI repainting 46 800 times would cost more than the arithmetic it narrates. `SparseSymmetricCG.Solve` deliberately reports NO fraction — an iteration count is not progress, since the residual falls at a rate nobody knows in advance and the cap is a stall detector.
- **A shared rule can be the WRONG rule for a new consumer, and the boundary layer is the one place `PolygonFan` must not be asked.** The repo's standing answer to "which way does a quad split" is the shorter 3D diagonal with a relative tie guard — right everywhere it is used, and precisely wrong for a boundary-layer prism, because a layer quad on a flat wall is an exact RECTANGLE whose two diagonals are mathematically equal. So the tie guard would fire on essentially every element of the stack rather than on the 408-of-960 minority it was written for, and the choice would fall to round-off; two prisms sharing a quad could then split it differently, which is non-conforming, and every solver would integrate over a gap it cannot see while returning a plausible answer. The rule there is combinatorial (Dompierre: the diagonal contains the smaller INPUT-SURFACE vertex index — symmetric in the two, so neighbours agree without communicating). The general lesson is not "prefer combinatorial rules": it is that **a shared rule's justification travels with it, and a consumer made entirely of that rule's documented degenerate case is a consumer it does not serve.**
- **The party that decides an interface's triangulation is the one that must be ASKED, not predicted.** The boundary layer's first design built the offset wall, handed it to the isotropic fill, and built prisms against the triangulation it had handed over — which is wrong on a box, because recovery works per planar PATCH exactly so that Delaunay may pick its own diagonal across a coplanar quad. Measured: 16 orphan boundary faces on a 20³ box, 266 on a sphere. The fix is to run the stage in two passes and read the interface triangulation BACK off the finished fill; the fill chooses, the stack conforms. Same shape as the `ExactSampleParameters` findings — restating what another stage decides is how all of these went wrong — and the payoff is that the conformity check becomes free: weld by exact position, and every interface triangle is then used by two elements and vanishes from the boundary, so **"every boundary face has a known tag" IS the statement that the two meshes conform**, with no second check to keep in step.
- **A quality report that cries wolf on correct output is worse than no report — but the partition that fixes it has an honest limit, and the limit must be stated.** `TetQuality`'s sliver rule (minimum dihedral below 10°) calls every legitimate boundary-layer element degenerate: a tetrahedron cut from a prism 0.15 mm thick and 1 mm wide has a minimum dihedral under a degree and a radius-edge ratio in the tens, and it is exactly right. So elements are partitioned by measured stretch and each half gets the rule that means something for it. Two sub-lessons. **A full whitening is a vacuous quality measure**: `M^{-1/2}` maps every non-degenerate tetrahedron to a well-shaped one, so scale only the THINNEST principal axis (up to the middle eigenvalue) — that restores a layer element while leaving a needle exactly as bad. And **no local geometric measure can separate a layer element from an accidental sliver**, because the two are AFFINELY EQUIVALENT — the stack element is four nearly-coplanar points too. What distinguishes them is whether the thin direction is shared with the neighbours and with the physics, which is intent, not geometry. Hence `AnisotropicCount` is reported BESIDE the stretched quality rather than instead of it: on a mesh nobody asked to be anisotropic, any value above zero is the finding.
- **A degeneracy guard must ask the assembly's OWN arithmetic, not restate it — the "one shared rule" lesson, in a solver.** The FEA solver refuses an element whose Jacobian is non-positive in double precision, because such an element contributes a stiffness of the wrong SIGN rather than an inaccurate one. (They exist: the tet mesher guarantees positive orientation by the EXACT predicate, and a sliver can satisfy that while its floating-point volume underflows — measured, two of 31 214 on a 100×10×10 beam.) The first version tested the CORNER triple product `(b−a)×(c−a)·(d−a)`; assembly integrates the **isoparametric Jacobian**, which is the same mathematical quantity by different arithmetic. They disagree in the last bits, so elements passed the guard and were then integrated as exactly zero — surfacing as a 10-node mid-edge node with **no stiffness at all**, on an element whose corner volume read a healthy 1.2e-15. Restating a test is how all three occurrences of this got it wrong (`ExactSampleParameters`' two, now this); ask the same code.
- **A convergence ORDER measured against a different model's answer stalls at the modelling difference.** The cantilever's tip deflection converges at a measured 1.86 for BOTH element types, which looks like a formulation defect and is not: the built-in end carries a stress singularity that caps the rate whatever the element. The theoretical rates only appear on a problem with no singularity, which is what the method of manufactured solutions is for — choose a smooth u, put it through the elasticity operator analytically for the body force that makes it exact, prescribe it on the whole boundary, and both orders then land on theory (2.00/1.00 linear, 3.03/2.02 quadratic in L2/energy). Two riders: the manufactured field must be CUBIC or a quadratic element reproduces it exactly and reports an infinite order; and the body-force load needs a degree-5 quadrature rule, because under-integrating a load caps the study at the QUADRATURE's order and looks identical to a bad element.
- **A quadrature rule that gets the TOTAL right can still produce a SINGULAR matrix, and the total is the check everyone reaches for.** A capacity (mass) matrix integrates `N·N`, degree 2p, two degrees above the conductivity/stiffness's `grad N · grad N` at 2(p−1) — so the rule that is exact for one is not exact for the other, and this is the shape of error that hides. Integrating a 4-node capacity with the conductivity's one-point centroid rule makes every one of its sixteen entries exactly `ρcV/16`: a rank-one matrix that cannot be factored, **whose entries sum to exactly ρcV**, the body's true heat capacity. Measured spread between entries: 0. So "does the capacity sum to the right thing" — the obvious sanity check, and the one a reviewer asks for — passes it. **The modal work generalized the failure and found the control that actually has teeth.** An n-point rule can only produce a matrix of RANK n, so the stiffness's rule gives a 4-node mass of rank 1 and a 10-node mass of rank 4 (measured, both), while `Σ_ij N_i N_j = (Σ_i N_i)² = 1` exactly makes the total right *whatever* the rule — the pass is structural, not luck. And the control is not "compare against a higher rule" but **the ROTATIONAL INERTIA**: a rigid rotation is linear in position, so every element order represents it exactly and `uᵀMu` must equal the tetrahedron's own `ωᵀIω`, which `MeshMassProperties`' closed-form polyhedral moments supply from another project entirely. The production rule agrees to **2.2e-16…9.4e-15 relative**; the one-point rule reports **−2.4e-27 against a true 1.4e-10** — no rotational inertia at all, because every entry being equal collapses `uᵀMu` to the square of the MEAN nodal value and the mean of a rotation about the centroid is exactly zero. A body with the right mass and no inertia would spin up under any torque and put every torsional frequency at infinity.
- **A single-node restraint is a STATIC idiom and it is wrong in dynamics.** A 3-2-1 scheme pins isolated degrees of freedom to remove rigid motions, and in statics that is sound: Saint-Venant confines the disturbance to the pinned node's own neighbourhood. In a modal solve the same pin creates a GENUINE mode — the whole body translating along the released direction while a few elements around the node deform — whose frequency is set by the mesh rather than by the part. Measured on a simply-supported 100×12×8 beam whose axial translation was pinned at one node: a spurious mode at **5 540 Hz**, sitting between the second and third bending modes, carrying 96% of the axial effective mass, and duly failing a comparison against the second bending frequency by 26%. The fix is a constraint the wanted modes satisfy IDENTICALLY rather than one that merely happens to be small: holding `u_x = 0` along the beam's own centroidal line removes the axial family and adds no bending stiffness, since pure bending has `u_x = −z·w′(x)` measured from the neutral axis. Same shape as the thermal fixtures' lesson — a restraint, like a fixture, can quietly change what is being measured.
- **Ritz values converge from the outside in, but NOT in lock step — so lock a contiguous prefix, never "whatever has converged".** Shift-and-invert Lanczos can have the second eigenvalue converged while the first is still moving, and accepting the converged ones in whatever order they arrive then returns eigenvalues 2 and 3 as "the two lowest". Measured on a 19 440-DOF cantilever asked for ONE mode: **4 997.9 Hz returned for a first bending mode of 834.9 Hz** — the second bending pair, a number entirely plausible on its own. Two further rules from the same work: full reorthogonalization is what stops ghost duplicates AND what makes a genuine multiplicity invisible (a single-vector Krylov space holds one vector per eigenspace, and a square shaft's two identical bending modes are the common case), so converged modes must be locked into the deflation set and the run restarted, targeting one more mode than asked for so a missed copy has a run to appear in; and the scratch buffer holding the next Lanczos vector's `M`-product must not be shared with the Rayleigh–Ritz step, which cost a whole iteration breaking down after exactly four steps rather than a wrong answer.
- **When the right-hand matrix goes indefinite, the free parameter is the METRIC, not the shift.** The modal solver puts a small negative shift under the spectrum because `K − σM = K + |σ|M` is then positive definite — which works ONLY because M is positive semi-definite. A geometric stiffness is indefinite (tension stiffens, compression softens, and one bending prestress does both), so no sign of σ makes `K − σ·Kg` reliably factorable, and the escalation loop is worse than inapplicable: multiplying a failing shift by 100 pushes it FURTHER from definiteness. The resolution is that `A^-1 B` is self-adjoint in the A inner product AND in the B inner product whenever A and B are symmetric, so Lanczos may run in either and the only requirement is that the one it runs in be definite. In buckling that is **K**, so the operator is `K^-1(-Kg)`, the factorized matrix is K itself (literally the static solver's factorization, on every model), and **the shift is exactly zero on every model** — because the substitution θ = 1/λ has already turned the wanted eigenvalue into an extreme one, which is the entire job shift-and-invert exists to do. ARPACK's "buckling mode" reaches the same place from the other side (`OP = (A − σM)^-1 A` with metric `B = A`) and REQUIRES σ ≠ 0, since at zero that operator is the identity; inverting the other matrix removes the requirement along with the choice. `LanczosEigen` needed exactly one generalization — the metric became a parameter separate from the right-hand matrix — and passing one matrix for both keeps the modal path bit-identical.
- **A residual that is a TOTAL cancellation has a floor of `eps·κ(K)`, and whether you hit it depends on whether the operator SMOOTHS.** Both eigensolvers measure `|Kφ − λBφ|/(|Kφ| + |λ||Bφ|)`, which at convergence is the round-off left by two nearly equal products. A modal solve escapes the floor because its Krylov vectors come out of `K^-1 M`, which suppresses the high-frequency content K amplifies before K ever sees it; a buckling solve's operator is `K^-1 Kg` and a geometric stiffness is derivative-like, so the two halves roughly cancel in frequency content and the vectors keep exactly the part that sets the floor. Measured on one slender column: every mesh up to 9 310 DOF reaches 1e-10 and a **23 166-DOF one stalls at 1.76e-9**, so the modal 1e-9 default would refuse an ordinary refinement — hence a 1e-7 buckling default, which costs nothing (an eigenvalue is accurate to the SQUARE of the residual over the gap; the same column accepted at 1e-5 returns 15 437.12 N against a finer mesh's 15 437.99 N). And **an empty eigen-result has two unrelated causes** — nothing wanted exists, or a candidate was there and the tolerance was in the way — which want opposite responses from a user and which an empty list cannot distinguish, so `LanczosEigen` reports the best candidate it did NOT accept and both solvers' refusals name which case they hit.
- **A verification fixture can make a convergence study measure nothing, and it does not look broken while it does.** Two occurrences in one session, both caught only because a measured order came out impossible rather than merely poor. **(a)** Grading a hollow cylinder's rings GEOMETRICALLY puts the nodes at equal intervals of `ln r` — the coordinate the exact conduction profile is linear in — *and* makes every ring-to-ring conductance equal, since each annular ring is a radially scaled copy of the last and a 2D conductance is scale-invariant. The exact nodal values then satisfy the discrete equations identically: 4.3e-14 on a 120 K drop at every refinement level, and a "convergence order" of **−2.50 then −1.27**, which is what the ratio of two round-off figures looks like when mistaken for a signal. **(b)** A CUBIC manufactured solution on a uniform mesh is reproduced exactly at the NODES by both element orders, because the finite-element stencil's truncation error is proportional to a fourth derivative a cubic does not have — 2.2e-13 on a field of scale 113. The same superconvergence appears in the 1D slab-with-generation case for both orders at once, for two *different* reasons (a parabola is in the quadratic space; the linear discrete equations reduce to a central difference a quadratic satisfies exactly), where a study asserting a refinement ratio of 4 measured **0.72**. The fixes are respectively uniform spacing, a quartic field, and measuring INSIDE the elements — where the ratios come out 4.00.
- **A measured order below theory may belong to the DOMAIN rather than to the elements, and which one is a measurement.** A hollow cylinder's conduction error converges at 1.28 (linear) and 2.00 (quadratic) against theory 2 and 3 — but the fixture's rings are polygons, so refining the mesh refines the *body*, and the boundary condition is constant along each chord where the true logarithmic profile is not. Refining the angular direction as `n²` instead of `n`, which drops the chord sagitta from O(h²) to O(h⁴), lifts the quadratic sequence to **2.28** and its finest error from 5.8e-4 to **1.1e-4** while leaving the linear one **unchanged** at 1.28 and 1.0e-3. So the two are limited by different things: the quadratic element is good enough that the geometry holds it back, and the linear one is still limited by its own approximation. Same shape as the cantilever's clamped-end singularity capping both orders at 1.86 — which is why a convergence ORDER is measured against a manufactured solution on a box, and an analytic curved case is reported as ACCURACY.
- **A "zero" that is a sum of large cancelling products has its floor set by the summation, not by the quantity.** Asserting that a rigid motion carries no strain energy, `U = ½uᵀKu` measured 2.3e-9 where the strain it came from measured 3.3e-16 — six orders apart, because U is a sum over elements of products of O(1) displacements with O(1e6) stiffnesses that cancel almost totally. The bar for each has to be relative to what a NON-rigid motion of the same size would produce (`E·(u/L)²·V` for the energy), not to the other assertion's scale. Reported relative: 1e-14 for the strain, 1e-15 for the energy.
- **An ambient-occlusion bake is a NEAREST-hit query, and a ray that ESCAPES cannot be pruned.** Two negatives worth keeping about the AO bake's 12.3 s. First, the obvious lever is not there: occlusion accumulates as `1 − t`, so a hit darkens in proportion to how close it is, and the any-hit early-out that "is this ray blocked" invites would be a different renderer (measured 0.055 darker over the vertices that see an occluder at all; a boolean bake is literally the search-radius-to-infinity limit of this one). Second, the exact lever that IS available — descending the nearer child box first — pays only in proportion to how OCCLUDED the geometry is, because an escaping ray never sets the bound below 1 and therefore prunes nothing: **1.19× on a gyroid lattice (mean occlusion 0.70), 1.04× i.e. nothing on a smooth CSG blob (mean occlusion 0.95)**. And the ray count is not a free knob either — halving 16 → 8 is 1.6–1.9× but changes **59 of the 87 rendered docs PNGs**, so it is a decision about appearance, not about performance. The no-less-honest-bake rule survives all three.
- **A block-pointer table costs 8 bytes per block whether or not the block is ever touched.** That is 0.2% of a block's samples and irrelevant up to a 1024³ grid — and 134 MB, allocated before a single sample is evaluated, at 4096³. Hence flat below a threshold, two-level above it, rather than one shape everywhere.
- **Interleaving a grid algorithm's phases is what drops the memory bound from O(n³) to O(n²)** — but the face ordering must then be reconstructed deliberately (bucket by whichever loop variable was outermost in the un-streamed version, then concatenate), or a "bit-for-bit identical" claim quietly holds for vertices and fails for faces.
- ****A fold COUNT is not a tessellation quality metric — measure the worst normal dot.** The cross-drilled bore wall that rendered as a visibly crumpled fan had **zero** strictly-inverted triangles before the fix and zero after; what was wrong was a worst facet-vs-surface dot of **0.0198**, an 88.9° sliver, which a fold count reports as a clean mesh. And the count is not the convergence test either: volume excess over the analytic value ran 61.19/18.60/13.40/11.25 at 32/64/128/256 segments-per-circle, i.e. ratios 3.29/**1.39/1.19** — stalling near 11 and never converging — against 76.20/21.49/5.97/1.82 (ratios 3.55/3.60/3.27, quadratic) after. **Ear clipping is STRUCTURALLY forced into a fan wherever a boundary run is exactly uv-collinear**, which is the normal case, not a pathology: both ring loops of an `ExtrudedSurface` band pull back to a bit-identical v (measured: distinct v bits = 1, 32/32 exactly-collinear uv triples), so `IsEar`'s `<= 0` rejects every corner along both chains and only the unrolled rectangle's four corners are clippable — so no change to the shortest-diagonal metric could have helped, and `Refine` then bisected the fan chords into slivers. Nor would the existing merge walk (`ZipBand`) have worked: it pairs chains by u, so a dense breakout curve against a coarse ring gets fanned from one far vertex and inverts where the curve turns back (measured uv cross −5.9e-4). A monotone sweep is the fix. A fold test must also never take the surface normal at a triangle's CENTROID.** A centroid sits a sagitta (~1e-3 at display quality) inside a curved surface, far past the 1e-6 inverse-evaluation tolerance, so `TryProjectPoint` fails and the assertion silently checks nothing — the first run of the mitered-fillet fold test reported zero folds when there were 808. Average the normals at the triangle's three **vertices**, which are exact surface points by construction.
- **A grid quad's diagonal is now chosen by GEOMETRY — and the tie guard was the hard half.** `PolygonFan` is the one rule (`Triangulated`, `SignedVolume`/`FaceFanStart`, `MeshMassProperties`, `MeshConnectedComponents`, `RenderMesh`, STL/3MF/AMF): a quad splits along its **shorter 3D diagonal**, n-gons keep the corner-0 fan. That resolves the recorded left-hand-thread finding exactly — the mirrored rod's deficit was 3× its twin's purely because mirroring swapped which diagonal a corner-0 fan picked, and the two now measure IDENTICALLY (1.5963 / 0.3997 / 0.1001 at 32/64/128 segments for both, against a mirrored 4.7643 / 1.1974 / 0.3002 before). **Three things the work taught.** (a) **An exact comparison would have reintroduced the same defect**: a great many grid cells have *mathematically equal* diagonals — every UV-sphere quad is mirror-symmetric about its own meridian — so their computed squares differ only in the last ulps, and **408 of the 960 quads of `UvSphere(40, 26)`** flip on round-off at a ratio of 1.000000000000, with the two triangulations equal to twelve digits of the volume deficit. Letting an ulp decide measurably perturbed decimation and remeshing; the fix is a *relative* tie guard (1e-12 on squared lengths, scale-free tier), and the collateral test failures vanished with it — which is what "a test needing a looser band is a bug" looks like when you follow it. (b) **The win is CONSISTENCY, not universally less error**: on a saddle cell the two triangulations bracket the surface (a twisted lofted column reads 244.37/255.27 at 16/12 and 249.66/250.34 at 256/192 — equal in magnitude, opposite in sign, both converging on 250), and one docs assertion was quietly reading the SIGN of the corner-0 fan rather than a property of the solid. (c) **`MeshMassProperties` was 1.5 out of 69.5 adrift** because it walked the half-edge arrays with its own corner-0 fan — a shared rule only holds if every consumer asks it, and the test that asserts volume/render/triangulate/mass-properties agree is what found it. Cost: **18 of 87 rendered docs PNGs move**, all in the non-planar-quad families (SDF/Surface Nets, threads, lofts).
- **A polygon's winding may be reversed freely; its FAN DIAGONAL may not.** Re-winding a quad `[a, b, c, d]` as `[d, c, b, a]` is the same cyclic polygon the other way round, so it is correct for orientation — and wrong for geometry, because everything downstream triangulates by fanning from vertex 0, which moves the split from a–c to b–d. On a skewed non-planar grid cell those are not equally good triangulations. `BRepTessellator`'s reversed-face flip did exactly this, so every *subtracted* tool's face got the worse diagonal: measured on an M8 B-Rep threaded hole (sheared helical grid, cell diagonal ratios up to 40:1) **5 544 of 30 912 facets faced INWARD at a worst facet-vs-surface agreement of −0.163**, while the identical geometry unsubtracted — a threaded rod — was clean at 0.99976. The fix is to rotate the reversal so vertex 0 stays put (`[a, d, c, b]`). The lesson generalizes past this bug: **a quality audit must fan polygons exactly as the consumer does**, because auditing the quad as a unit would have reported this mesh perfect.
- **The general algorithm beat two special cases, again.** The strip path's two documented gaps — a band end sampled at more than two points (a curved cross edge) and a band whose chains meet at a point — were both closed by replacing the rung-counting split with the **monotone stack sweep** already used for slabs, which handles a tied run of end vertices and a single apex as its ordinary start and finish. The load-bearing detail is the tie-break: take the LAST of the tied minimum run and the FIRST of the tied maximum run, so a whole tied run lands on ONE chain — split across both, the merge interleaves the sides at equal keys and the sweep is handed collinear points to triangulate. Every band in the docs tessellates bit-identically either way (all 52 rendered PNGs unchanged), which is what a generalization done right looks like. **Neither shape is reachable from the `Shape` API yet** — `Sphere − Box` corner cuts and cone-apex cuts are refused earlier by the exact boolean, and eighteen further candidates reached neither — so they are covered by hand-built faces in `TrimmedBandGapTests`.
- **Both loud trimmed-face refusals are UNREACHABLE from the `Shape` API, and that was worth finding out rather than guessing.** Pole-bounded single-chain bands with holes and |winding| > 1 loops are blocked one layer up, by the exact B-Rep boolean. The near miss is instructive: a latitude cut DOES give a sphere a pole-bounded cap with a single winding loop, and drilling that cap off-axis does NOT put a hole in it — the boolean re-splits so the bore's rim lands on the two-ring band below, which the slab sweep already handles. Cutting lower, or the same on a cone or a torus, fails earlier with "produced an unclosed solid"; |winding| > 1 needs a curve wrapping a periodic surface twice, which only a helical intersection gives and those are refused before tessellation. `TrimmedFaceRefusalTests` locks the verdict AND drives both refusals on hand-built faces, so the backstop's messages cannot rot while nothing reaches them.
- **A non-converging error whose sign FLIPS is a fixed floor, not a tolerance.** A bore drilled into an extruded SIDE face misses the inscribed-ngon volume by −7.4e-4 / −5.3e-5 / +4.7e-5 / +6.5e-5 at 32/64/128/256 segments, while the identical hole in the top cap lands within 1e-13. The cause is not the trimmed triangulation, which was the standing assumption: the rim on the side face is a **57-sample `PolylineCurve3d` baked in by the marching tracer**, because plane-as-a-bounded-extrusion ∩ cylinder is not one of `SurfaceIntersection`'s analytic pairs, and a 57-gon is a floor no `segmentsPerCircle` can lower — the sign flips when the analytic reference's own n-gon crosses it. Same shape of finding as the cross-drilled housing's breakout curves. The fix is a promotion in `SurfaceIntersection`, not a sampling rule.
- **Refinement can make a mesh WORSE — resolved by interior rows in the base triangulation, and the numbers prove the diagnosis.** The base has to carry the surface's curvature itself: trimmed bands now take the natural grid's own sample rows into their BASE (`RowedStrip`/`RowedPeriodicBand`/`RowedPoleFan` — level paths threading between scallops, anchored on existing boundary vertices, never inventing a boundary point; full-period rows with closure duplicates for winding bands; seam chords pre-split with bit-identical twins since a seam is an unrolling artifact internal to the face; a uv-area identity as the closing guard with the rowless sweep as fallback, so the rowed path can never be worse). The drilled sphere `Sphere(10) − Cylinder(3, 40)` went **43 948 facets / 12 folds / worst −0.2022 → 3 244 / 0 folds / 0.9994** (32/24), stopped refusing at 128/96, and converges at ratios 4.35 / 5.08 — it is now a corpus member with an analytic napkin-ring volume row. Two rules that made it work: **`Refine`'s step metric is per-axis (max-norm), never the 2-norm** — the natural grid's own cell diagonal spans one step in EACH axis and measures √2 under a 2-norm, so refinement would bisect the very grid that defines the quality bar; and **pole-fan edges are refinement-exempt** — the pole's u is arbitrary, so a fan edge's uv u-span is an artifact, not curvature (refining a *flat* vase disk's fan bent it into 467 folds at −1.0). With rows in place Refine measures IDLE on 16 of 19 corpus members' trimmed faces and keeps only its honest duty: residual coarse columns between honest samples.
- **The corpus quality gate is `TessellationCorpusQualityTests` + `TessellationQuality`** — 30 constructions (drilled/cross-drilled plates, spherical cavities, a drilled sphere, threaded rods and holes, lofts, shells, drafts, mitered and whole-solid fillets, chamfers, vases, partial revolves, sweeps, tori, cones, curved-face shells and drafts, sketch pockets, Bézier engraving, wedges) audited facet-by-facet against their exact surfaces at 16/8, 48/24 and 96/48, on **unwelded per-face polygons** (`BRepTessellator.TessellateByFace`, an internal seam — welding is what destroys the facet→face attribution the measurement needs). One floor for every surface family, `cos(3·2π/n)`: three natural grid steps, the allowance being for facets where two INDEPENDENTLY sampled boundaries meet. A per-family floor tuned to what each happened to measure would pass everything and protect nothing. It also separates **degenerate slivers** (facet height below `1e-13 × the face's extent` — relative, since an absolute epsilon on a cross product is an area threshold) from **folds**: "the normal is noise" and "the normal points the wrong way" are different defects with different fixes, and both get asserted. First run caught three defects; all three are fixed (the third by the interior-rows upgrade above). Now **30 constructions** — the drilled sphere joined once rows took it past the floor at every density, and the curved-corner work added a shelled cup, a shelled cone, a shelled elbow, a drafted cylinder and a variable-radius fillet, all clean at all three densities with `KnownOffSurface` still empty. The one construction still below the floor, `Box(20,20,20) − Sphere(12)` (the cavity breaks out of all six faces), went **101 246 facets / 266 folds / worst −0.2426 → 4 608 / 0 folds / 0.7024** at 48/24 and tessellates at 96/48 where it used to refuse; what remains is a narrow column at each hole rim's u-extreme where the rim tangent goes vertical and no level path can anchor — locked with committed baselines in `SpherePiercingEverySide_HasNoFoldsAndABoundedResidual`, residual filed in todo.md.
- **"All the rendered PNGs are byte-identical" is the right oracle for a change that must not move geometry, and the WRONG one for a change that legitimately re-decomposes it.** Carrier clipping stops a face being split along geometry its partner does not share, so face counts fall by design (18 → 11, 20 → 13 on the two ambient-occlusion fixtures) while the solid stays the same solid — same polygon count, same volume to nine decimals. But the triangulation of a face-with-one-hole is not the triangulation of eight rectangles, so the flat render mesh's vertices sit in different places and their baked occlusion differs: **7 of 106 docs PNGs moved, every one a B-Rep-boolean scene, none with a changed silhouette**, and the two committed AO fingerprints had to be re-taken. What survives as an oracle is what the faces MEAN — fewer, better-shaped faces at the same volume is an improvement, a changed silhouette would not be — so the argument has to be made in the commit rather than delegated to a byte comparison. Measure the delta, look at both images, and say which it is. **And never read the output directory of a build that has not said it is done** — the first DocsGen pass was polled mid-run, reported four, and three more landed after; two further runs from a clean checkout reproduced all seven bit-identically, so the renderer was deterministic and the *observation* was the thing that was wrong.
- **Zero-area triangles are not merely useless — near a tangency they are actively WRONG.** A sliver clipped along a boundary curve takes that curve's binormal as its normal, so wherever a trim curve is tangent to a neighbouring face (every fillet's tangency line) its orientation is decided by rounding, and no epsilon fixes it. See the trimmed-face tier order above.
- **Refinement is not a convergence mechanism.** The midpoint-split pass terminates on a monotone-decrease rule, so wherever that rule cuts a cascade the mesh keeps a coarse patch forever. A tessellation whose accuracy depends on refinement has no convergence guarantee — measured, the ear-clipped fillet's volume error stopped improving past curveSamples 96 and then wandered. Get the base triangulation right; use refinement only for genuine curvature between honest samples. **This rule is now enforced structurally in the trimmed tiers**: interior rows put the natural grid into the base, and the corpus measures refinement idle wherever the rows engage — where refinement still visibly inflates a mesh, the finding is a missing row path, not a refinement bug.
- **A recursion whose depth is a property of the INPUT has no "big enough" stack.** Learned from the (now deleted) BSP boolean, and the rule outlives the code: `CsgNode`'s splitting plane was the first polygon's plane, so a convex body — a sphere above all — built an essentially degenerate chain of depth O(polygons). Two 32k-triangle spheres overflowed the CLR stack **inside `Build`** (not `Invert`, as this file long assumed) and killed the process; measured threshold, a 3000-node chain built in 419 ms and a 4000-node chain crashed. Rewriting all five walks with explicit stacks fixed the crash and bought honesty rather than speed — the completed run returned an *open* shell in 74.9 s — which is what settled the decision to retire it.
- **A batch of refinement points must be INDEPENDENT, or the bound that makes refinement finite silently evaporates.** Delaunay refinement terminates because a tetrahedron's circumcentre is, by the empty-circumsphere property, at least its circumradius from every existing vertex — so points pack no denser than the local radius and a bounded domain holds boundedly many. That guarantee is against the triangulation *as it was when the circumcentre was computed*. Inserting a batch straight off the priority queue voids it: the second point has no relationship to the first, so two candidates land arbitrarily close, make a sliver, and feed the next round. Measured on a 4-unit box asked for 1.5-unit elements: 200-, 800- and 3000-point budgets all exhausted without converging; requiring each accepted point to be half a circumradius from the others accepted in the same pass converges to 836 elements identically at every budget. The same shape of error as seeding a priority queue off incomplete quadrics — a quantity used after the thing it depends on has moved.
- **A refinement rule that responds to a defect must be LOCAL, or the response is the next defect.** The boundary-recovery answer to a face that failed to lie on the surface was to split every sub-triangle of the PATCH it touched; on a box that quadruples a whole face per round, so one interior insertion near a wall cascaded the entire surface and exhausted a 500 000-point budget. Ruppert's encroachment ball — split exactly the sub-triangles whose diametral ball contains the offending vertex — is the smallest region that provably has to change. It also needs a size floor: without one, a circumcentre sitting essentially ON the surface encroaches forever, each split halving the balls while the point stays inside.
- **Seed a priority queue only after the quantity it is keyed on is COMPLETE.** Folding QEM seeding into the quadric-accumulation loop keys the whole initial queue off *partially accumulated* quadrics. Every mesh still came out closed, manifold and at exactly the requested face count — the sole symptom was a **2.4× worse approximation error at light decimation**, where the cheapest (earliest) choices dominate. A committed per-case error baseline is what caught it; without one it would have shipped invisibly.
- **Feature detection reads the mesh you give it, not the surface you meant.** A coarse tessellation of a smooth surface has large dihedrals — `UvSphere(12, 8)` facets meet at ~30°, so the remesher's default 30° feature angle pins much of it and refinement cannot help (splitting an edge leaves the halves coplanar with their parents, so the dihedral is unchanged). Pass 0, or a larger angle, when remeshing tessellated curvature; the default protects CAD creases, which is the more dangerous failure to get wrong.
- **Where the standard algorithm exists, take it over the reference library's heuristic.** g3's `MinimalHoleFill` is four iterative edge-flip passes whose own comments describe strong ordering effects, non-convergence, hard pass caps to stop oscillation, and a forced interior-vertex-removal stage with a debugger break in it. The Barequet–Sharir/Liepa dynamic program solves the same problem deterministically and globally optimally in O(n³)/O(n²) — trivial at realistic rim lengths. Its dihedral measure is `atan2(|a×b|, a·b)` on **raw** normals: exact at any magnitude, no normalization and no epsilon anywhere.
- **Degeneracy guards on areas and determinants must be RELATIVE, never absolute** (the BSP lesson, occurrences two through five — `SketchRegion`'s bounding-box reject compares SQUARED distances, so its conservativeness slack is relative too: `1e-15`, about 9 ulps against the bound's own ≤4 ulps of arithmetic error, the scale-free tier and never the weld tier; `MeshDecimator` was the third and fourth — `MeshDecimator` was the third and fourth: it normalized face normals with `TryNormalize(Tolerance.Default)`, an absolute 1e-9 applied to a *cross product* i.e. an area, so below ~1e-4 scale every face read as degenerate, contributed no quadric, and decimation became arbitrary — measured **91% volume error** on a 1e-5-scale sphere, now 3.1%; its normal-flip guard likewise compared a squared area against an absolute 1e-24. Both are now `1e-13 × extent`, the exact boolean's scale-free tier, and `Remesher` was written to that rule from the start — every threshold relative to target edge length, every degeneracy guard to its square, and guards reading the **sign** of the dot of two unnormalized cross products): `Sketch`'s enclosed-area, arc-chord and `ArcThrough` circumcenter guards were absolute 1e-12 and therefore wrong in *both* directions — a legitimate 1 µm × 0.5 µm pocket was rejected as enclosing no area, while a 1000 × 1e-10 sliver passed; three metre-scale points 1e-10 off a line built a radius-1.25e15 circle. They now scale by extent² / magnitude² (`Sketch.RelativeDegeneracy`), because an area is quadratic in scale. Deliberately still absolute, with reasons in code: sketch closure (the 1e-9 weld tier) and sweep angular guards (radians are dimensionless).
- **An exact predicate applied to a quantity that is round-off answers CONFIDENTLY and WRONG — and a tangent evaluated AT a tangency is exactly such a quantity.** The curved 2D arrangement orders the edges at a node by the exact `Orient2dSign` of their departure directions, which is right everywhere except where it matters most: at a tangency the two directions differ only by arithmetic noise. Measured, a disc tangent to a plate's straight edge from outside gave the arc a departure of **(−1.22e-16, −1)** — the x sign is nothing but the error in `sin(π)` — which put it on the wrong side of the plate's exactly vertical edge, so the tightest-turn walk closed **no face at all** and the union came back **empty**, not merely inaccurate. Only the CURVATURE carries information there, so a second pass re-orders each cyclic run of tangentially tied departures by it (cyclic because a tangency along +x puts one departure at the very start of the fan and its partner at the very end). **The tie band is DERIVED, not chosen**: a vertex may sit up to the snap tolerance from the true tangency point, and displacing a point by δ along a circle of radius r rotates its radial — hence its tangent — by δ/r = δ·|κ|, so the band is `snap·max(|κ₁|, |κ₂|)` plus a few-ulp arithmetic floor. That floor is all that remains for two straight edges, so genuinely distinct line directions stay decided exactly. Same shape as design.md §5's `DepartureAngle` note: **Shewchuk exactness is exactness about the coordinates you hand it.**
- **A closed curve's END must be its START, exactly — a parameterization that merely evaluates to the same place is not closed.** `sin(2π)` is not 0 in doubles, so a full-turn arc built as `centre + r·(cos θ, sin θ)` at θ₀ and θ₀ + 2π lands ~2e-16·r apart. That gap is not cosmetic: a +x parity ray whose ordinate falls INSIDE it counts the seam piece's two endpoints on opposite sides, and a point measurably inside a disc reads as outside — found by a disc ∩ rectangle that returned empty where the analytic answer was a 61.4-unit circular segment. Two rules fix it and both are load-bearing: a full turn's end point IS its start point (an exact substitution, not a snap), and an arc's first and last y-monotone piece take their ordinate from the **stored endpoints**, never re-derived from the angle — which is also what makes a chain's parity consistent across every joint, since the next edge starts at exactly this edge's stored end. The same shape of bug as the boolean's canonical-crossing rule: recomputation must be bit-reproducible, not merely close.
- **When a "boolean instability" and a "tessellation artifact" are both plausible, PROVE which by asking the mesh, not by tightening an epsilon.** The 2D boolean carried a filed defect for a long time — "leaves ~1e-7-area pinholes at near-tangency; a cell-classification fix, not an epsilon one" — repro'd by a 64×48 torus silhouette seen side-on. It is the opposite: 780 of 780 probe points inside that 1.45e-5 hole are covered by **zero** facets, tested with the exact `Orient2d` over every triangle, so the union returned is the correct union of what the boolean was given, and the *tessellated solid's own shadow* has the hole. In the band |z| ∈ [r·cos(π/n_minor), r] — the minor polygon's scallop, 4.28e-3 deep at n = 48 — the discrete tube only reaches that height near its minor-polygon VERTICES, and the major discretization breaks that thin band into lenses that need not overlap (the hole measured 1.16e-3 deep, a quarter of the scallop). It is not systematic either: holes at 64×48 and none at 32×24, 96×72, 128×96, 64×96 or 128×48, because whether two neighbouring lenses overlap is an *alignment* question, not a tolerance one. **A silhouette is the shadow of the mesh you give it, not of the surface you meant** — the remesher's feature-detection rule in new clothes. The test now asserts the strong form (every hole is uncovered by every facet), which is what separates "the boolean lost material" from "the tessellation has no material there".
- **Splitting a face by curves ONE AT A TIME and splitting it by all of them AT ONCE are different algorithms, and which one is needed is a property of the curves, not a preference.** A cascade works — and is what B-Rep booleans have always done — for exactly one reason: a curve that enters and leaves through the face's boundary closes its own arrangement, so a later curve meets an earlier one's segments as ordinary boundary edges of the fragment it is splitting and curve–curve crossings come out for free. It structurally cannot work the moment a curve TERMINATES INSIDE the face, because the FIRST curve applied has no partner segments to end on: its arrangement has a dangling edge, and a dangling edge cannot be traced. `FaceSplitter.SplitByCurves` therefore owns the routing and `SplitByCurve` is the one-curve call into it, so the incumbent path is reached *through* the general one rather than beside it. **The gate that picks between them is "a curve stops strictly inside the face WHERE ANOTHER CURVE IS THERE TO MEET IT", and the partner clause is the load-bearing half** — it separates a CLIPPED curve, whose terminus is a real corner the neighbouring face's curve continues from, from a TRACER-TRUNCATED one, whose terminus is an artefact of the marching step and has nothing to meet. Tracer truncation is common, so a gate without the clause engages the new path on ordinary geometry: measured on `Torus(12, 4) − plane − Ø3 bore`, a documented refusal either way, routing it to the arrangement made it fail as "two band-bottom boundaries with no top between them" instead of as the boolean's own "unclosed solid" — one stage earlier and less informatively. **An algorithm that can only trade one refusal for another should not be reached at all.** Three more rules the simultaneous path needed. **A junction where two curves meet END TO END is not a crossing and must not be reached for by loosening a transversality test** — they touch, and whether a crossing test fires there depends on the angle they happen to meet at; coincident endpoints are matched explicitly, at the seam tier. And **the gate is tested before a single coedge moves**: `SplitEdge` patches every loop using the edge, including neighbouring faces', so a split that refused halfway would leave them half-edited — the rim-surgery all-or-nothing rule, in the boolean core.
- **A loop WRAPS a periodic surface by net DRIFT, not by SPAN — and a span test is wrong in the direction that hides.** Three sites decided "is this a band boundary?" by whether the pulled loop's u reached three quarters of the period; a contractible loop can reach that far and come back, and the case that found it is not exotic — a threaded rod's end-chamfer facet on its cone spans **272°** and closes with a net drift of **0.02 rad**. What follows is not an inaccuracy but a category error at each consumer: `FaceSplitter.TraceFaces` hunts a partner boundary to pair the facet with, wrap-splitting lets a wrapping cut fabricate a phantom band out of it, and `BrepBoolean.ProbePoint` takes the band path — which probes halfway toward the surface's own v domain edge, correct for a ring-bounded band and catastrophic here, because the probe lands OUTSIDE the fragment and the facet is classified away as if it were the other solid's material. It is now one rule, `FaceGeometry.LoopWrapsPeriod`, asked by all three (the "one shared rule only holds if every caller asks it" lesson, applied before the third copy could drift). The span stays as the cheap first half of an AND: no loop can drift a period without spanning one.
- **Two units that are each individually right cannot be caught by any test — only by making them ONE.** `Materials.Steel.Density` said 7.85e-9 (tonne/mm³) while `PartMassProperties`' remarks said 7.85e-6 (kg/mm³), a factor of 1000, and BOTH shipped, in docs, for as long as there were two `Material`-shaped ideas. Nothing could catch it: neither figure is wrong, only the pairing is, and a kernel that carries no units at runtime has nothing to compare. Three rules came out of settling it (design.md §2). **(a) A quantity is either something an EQUATION consumes or something a REPORT prints, and only the second can be converted after the fact** — so the convention belongs wherever a conversion has nowhere to live (an FEA mass matrix must balance a stiffness in MPa against a length in mm, and the factor would have to reappear in the gravity load, the heat capacity, the frequency, the buckling load and the transient time constant), and the readable units become ACCESSORS. A consistent internal unit with a documented accessor is not the same thing as two conventions. **(b) A stored constant must be ASSERTED in the form a human can check** — the catalogue stores tonne/mm³ and the tests assert kg/m³ against the datasheet, because comparing 7.85e-9 with 7.85e-9 verifies typing, not physics (the same test caught a quoted thermal diffusivity of 68.7 that the catalogue's own rows make 69.03). **(c) The way to fix a duplicated convention is to delete one of the types, not to add a note** — a cross-reference warning had been left in `PartMassProperties` as a stopgap and it did nothing, because a reader who already believes their own figure does not go looking. And the regression oracle for the merge is worth naming: **every FEA verification number depends on density**, so if a unit had slipped anywhere, the cantilever deflection, the modal frequencies (∝ 1/√ρ), the buckling loads and the thermal transients would have moved. None did.
- **The epsilon ladder** (audited repo-wide; every site is documented in place): new epsilons must pick a tier — **1e-9 absolute weld** (= `Tolerance.Default.Linear`: exactly-constructed shared geometry — tessellation welds, seam breaks, clone dedupe) / **1e-7 seam** (geometry built independently on two sides of one curve — `SealSeams`, boundary-vs-grid matching; `MeshWelder`'s T-junction zip expresses this tier **relatively**, as 1e-7 × the soup's extent, since every source of independent-construction error — a chord's sagitta, a float32-quantized STL's cracks — is proportional to the model, and an absolute form was wrong in both directions away from unit scale) / **1e-6 inverse-evaluation** (named: `FaceGeometry.InverseEvaluationTolerance` — Gauss–Newton pullback; three decades looser than weld, do not tighten; the 1e-7 seam tier is likewise named `FaceGeometry.SeamTolerance`, and `SurfaceIntersection`'s marching constants live together in `TracerSettings` because they must be tuned as a set) / **scale-free algorithmic guards** (relative machine-epsilon degeneracy tests, exact-zero division guards, exact-zero *semantic* tests — these deliberately bypass `Tolerance` and say so in a comment). Never invent a fresh literal. Shewchuk `Predicates2d` error-bound constants are the algorithm — off limits.

Two perf lessons worth keeping: **the AoS→SoA transpose must be written against raw references** — four span bounds checks per point cost more than a cheap kernel saves, and going through indexers halved `Sdf.Box` throughput; and **JIT tiering makes single-warm-up benchmarks meaningless** (the same code measured 147, 314 and 548 Mpts/s across runs — use a wall-clock warm-up *budget*, not a warm-up count; confirmed a second time by the decimator port, which one warm-up call measured at 1.4× *slower* and a 1.5 s warm-up budget plus best-of-9 measured at 0.84× — same code, same machine, opposite conclusion). **Sparse lazy grids**: `Sampled(region, cell, lazy: true)`'s block table is **flat while that is free and grouped once it is not** — up to 64³ blocks (a 1024³ grid, 2 MB of pointers) it stays flat so existing models keep exactly the lookup they had (6.2–6.9 Mpts/s before and after); above it, blocks group into 16³-block super-blocks whose slot tables allocate on first touch, turning a 4096³ grid's 134 MB of *never-touched* pointers into 32 KB plus 32 KB per super-block visited. The `int`-addressing cap moved out of `GridFrame` into `RequireDenseAddressable` (dense bakers only) and its message names the lazy overload as the way out, so a domain the dense path cannot allocate at all now simply works: at a 0.0012 cell (11.7 G samples, 87 GB of doubles) the dense bake throws and the sparse grid answers in 80.6 MB; at 0.01 (20.3 M samples) dense is 18 816 ms/156.5 MB against 8 976 ms/28.9 MB sparse. Output is bit-for-bit unchanged **including face ordering**, because quads go into per-axis buckets keyed by whichever loop variable was outermost in the dense emission pass and are concatenated at the end (measured: res 96 **39.9 → 15.6 ms**, 40.9 → 19.9 MB; res 256 **735.5 → 258.8 ms**, 562 → 145 MB; res 384 **1922.7 → 747.5 ms**, 1842 → 289 MB — and all 47 rendered docs PNGs are byte-identical across the change). **Sketch fields are batched**: `IPlanarRegion` carries a defaulted batch overload `SignedDistance(x, y, distances)`; `ExtrudedRegionSdf` asks the region **once per distinct (x, y)** (a prism's field does not vary along z and bulk consumers sample z fastest — an exact memoization, the run test being an *identity* comparison, not a tolerance), and `SketchRegion` flattens its segments into structure-of-arrays form with lane-wise line/full-circle kernels transcribed term for term from `LineSeg.Distance`/`ArcSeg.Distance`, a conservative bounding-box reject in front of the bézier kernel (a cubic costs 17 curve evaluations plus 8 Newton steps), and a y-bucket index over the ray-parity pieces. Every one is a pure restructuring, locked by golden bit-hashes of the plain segment loop plus batch-vs-scalar bit equality (measured: a 14-segment plate **3.78 → 10.92 Mpts/s** scalar and **15.8 → 7.9 ms** to polygonize; a 108-segment engraving-shaped profile **0.18 → 0.81 Mpts/s** and **107.2 → 10.2 ms**). **Partial arcs and cubic béziers now have lane-wise kernels too, and neither could be a transcription** — which is the institutional-memory part. An arc's in-sweep test is `Math.Atan2`, with no bit-exact vector form; the replacement reads the signs of the cross products against the sweep's two boundary rays (`c₀ ≥ 0 && c₁ ≤ 0` up to a half turn, the same pair OR'd beyond it, because past π the *complement* is the narrow wedge; at exactly π the forms coincide, so the branch has no boundary of its own). Deciding the same predicate by different arithmetic means the two agree only away from the flip, so the kernel **refuses near it**: `c₀ = |o|·sin(δ)` and `c₁ = |o|·sin(δ − span)`, so requiring both to exceed `1e-9·|o|` puts the point a nanoradian off either boundary ray — five orders outside everything the scalar path can contribute (`Atan2`'s few ulps of a result bounded by π, plus the subtraction and the reduction by the *double* `2*PI`, both bounded because an arc is only classified vectorizable when |from| ≤ 64; the `%` itself is exact) — and any lane inside that **certainty band** sends its whole block back to `Atan2`. So the contract stays the strong one: **bit-identical for every input, not a bounded deviation**. A bounded deviation was available and simpler (the two branches of an arc's distance are continuous across the sweep boundary, so disagreeing there costs only O(r·ε)) and was rejected because this field's *sign* drives boolean classification kernel-wide. Note which inputs land in the band: a segment endpoint, shared bit-for-bit with its neighbour, sits exactly ON a boundary ray, so the cases that most want exactness take the exact path by construction. The bézier kernel's lesson is smaller and sharper: its Newton stage's `break` on a vanishing derivative is reproduced by **masking the WRITE** to the refined parameter with a sticky per-lane flag, NOT by letting stopped lanes iterate on — Newton from a stopped point is not a fixed point here, since a vanishing `g′` makes the step infinite and the clamp would walk the lane to 0 or 1. Its other half is structural: the 17 scan points are the same for every query, so they are baked at construction instead of re-evaluated per call. Third rule, from the same work: **a lane-wise kernel behind a conservative skip should substitute +∞ for the skipped lanes, not reuse the skip's proof** — blending +∞ before the min-fold is what "skip" means to a running minimum and costs one select, whereas "the reject proves they cannot lower the minimum anyway" makes the two paths' agreement rest on a second argument about the computed value's error. Measured on the batch entry (win-x64, `Vector<double>.Count` = 4, builds alternated within one sitting): a stadium **14.31 → 31.90 Mpts/s** (2.23×), an all-cubic 12-petal outline **0.41 → 0.56** (1.37×) and **10.6 → 9.3 ms** to polygonize, the plate **12.64 → 15.35**; nothing measured loses. Pipeline: exact-predicate Bowyer–Watson Delaunay of the surface vertices → classification by winding number at each element's centroid → **boundary recovery** (refine until every inside/outside separating face lies on the input surface, verified and named when it does not) → optional Delaunay refinement to a radius-edge target and/or a `Func<Vector3d, double>` sizing field (an `Sdf` composes directly). Outputs `TetMesh` (SoA, every element positively oriented — checked EXACTLY, so `Volume` is a sum of positive terms with no cancellation), per-element region ids from multi-body input, and boundary facets each naming the input triangle they subdivide (`TetFacet.SourceTriangle` — the seam for boundary conditions and B-Rep face identity). `QuadraticTetMesh.From` is the 10-node layer as a PURE function of the linear mesh (mid-edge nodes keyed on the canonical `(min, max)` corner pair so elements sharing an edge share the node; corner indices unchanged so corner data transfers with no mapping; Abaqus C3D10 / VTK ordering). `TetQuality` reports minimum dihedral BESIDE radius-edge because bounding radius-edge provably cannot exclude slivers, plus histograms and `SliverCount`. **Three design decisions, each reached by a failure** (design.md §3b): recovery works per planar PATCH not per input triangle (a Delaunay triangulation picks its own diagonal across a coplanar quad, so demanding the input triangle never converges — a unit cube exhausted a 500 000-point budget); **classification comes BEFORE the boundary** (an exactly-coplanar quad makes the tetrahedralization contain a FLAT tetrahedron whose four faces are both diagonals at once, so area coverage reads exactly 2.0000× and refines forever — 40 of 72 patches on a 12×6 UV sphere; deriving the boundary from an independently-decided classification has no tie to break, and it took the suite from 95 s to 3 s); and refinement follows **Ruppert's rule in Ruppert's ORDER** (an encroaching circumcentre is never inserted, the sub-triangle is split instead — inserting first and repairing after cascades). Note the irony, which is the useful part: remeshing is the natural surface-quality prep and v1 recovery wants exactly the structure it removes — mesh the tessellation directly and control size with a sizing field. Docs: `docs/examples/fea-meshing.md`. **Anisotropic boundary layers landed** (`BoundaryLayerSpec` on `TetMeshOptions`; design.md §3b; docs `examples/fea-meshing.md`): a graded stack — first-layer thickness, growth ratio, layer count, the standard controls — marched INWARD from a wall selected by a **`Facets` predicate, the same selector a boundary condition uses**, so a wall is named once. **It adds no volume algorithm**: the nodes are marched first, and what is left over is bounded by an ordinary closed triangle mesh (the offset wall plus the non-wall faces trimmed back to the stack's rim) that `TetMesher` already fills, so the volume identity, exact orientation, determinism and the refusal culture are inherited rather than restated; the stack's elements are emitted FIRST, so `[0, BoundaryLayerReport.ElementCount)` names them. Nodes march along an ANGLE-WEIGHTED per-node average (the `MeshSdf` pseudonormal convention) — a facet normal would move a shared vertex to several places and tear the layer along every edge where two facets disagree, which on a curved wall is every edge — and a rim node's direction is PROJECTED out of every non-wall plane it touches, so the stack's side wall is genuinely part of the part's surface rather than approximately part of it (how far the projection had to turn is then checked, 60° by default; a rim landing on a CURVED neighbour is refused by name). **Three decisions carry it.** (a) **The prism-to-tet diagonal rule is COMBINATORIAL, and `PolygonFan`'s shorter-3D-diagonal rule would be wrong here** — a layer quad on a flat wall is an exact rectangle whose two diagonals are mathematically equal, so the choice would fall to round-off on essentially every element of the stack (the 408-of-960 UV-sphere trap, except that a boundary layer is made ENTIRELY of that degenerate case). Dompierre's rule — a quad's diagonal contains whichever of its two base vertices has the smaller INPUT-SURFACE index — is symmetric in the two, so neighbours agree without communicating. (b) **The stage runs in TWO passes because the party that decides the interface triangulation is the FILL, not the layer**: boundary recovery works per planar PATCH precisely because Delaunay picks its own diagonal across a coplanar quad, so handing over an offset wall and assuming it comes back triangulated the same way is wrong on a box and wrong SILENTLY. March the columns, hand over the surface, read the interface triangulation back off the finished fill, build the prisms on that. The weld is by exact position, which makes the conformity check free: each interface triangle is then used by two elements and vanishes from the combined boundary, so **"every boundary face has a known tag" IS the statement that the two meshes conform**. (c) **`TetQuality` PARTITIONS rather than judging** — its sliver rule would call every legitimate layer element degenerate, and a report that cries wolf on correct output is worse than none — so `SliverCount` and the radius-edge figures cover the ISOTROPIC elements only while the stretched ones are counted (`AnisotropicCount`/`MaxStretch`) and measured in their own metric (`MinStretchedDihedralDegrees`: the min dihedral after un-stretching along the element's thinnest principal axis — only that axis, because a FULL whitening maps every non-degenerate tetrahedron to a well-shaped one and so carries no signal, whereas scaling one axis restores a layer element and leaves a needle exactly as bad). A mesh with nothing stretched reports what it always did, number for number. **The honest limit is stated rather than implied**: a layer element and an accidental sliver are AFFINELY EQUIVALENT (the stack element is four nearly-coplanar points too), so no purely local geometric measure separates them — what distinguishes them is whether the thin direction is shared with the neighbours and with the physics, which is intent, not geometry. **The frozen interface is the consequence to plan around**: once the stack has elements against its inner face, optional refinement must not insert a vertex there, so Ruppert's encroachment blocks interior points inside those triangles' diametral balls — on a plain two-triangles-per-face box that is the whole interior and NOTHING refines (measured: 72 declined points, a 6-element core). That is the standing rule of boundary-layer meshing showing through — **the surface mesh sets the layer's in-plane element size** — and it is REPORTED (`RefinementBlockedByFrozenBoundary`) rather than discovered. Note which paths are frozen: the two OPTIONAL ones (both merely decline, and the loop is still bounded without them), while boundary RECOVERY is deliberately left free, since a frozen patch that must be split for the mesh to conform at all is a real failure the layer's own interface check names better. Refusals, in the order they fire: a per-facet FOLD (the wall turning faster than the stack is tall), a trimmed face TURNING INSIDE OUT (two flat walls swapping places across a thin part), a global SELF-INTERSECTION, and non-positive leftover volume. **Nothing reachable from a real body exercises the self-intersection net**, and the reason is structural rather than lucky — two flat walls closing never CROSS, they swap places, and two parallel sheets that have swapped places are still parallel, so the face between them inverts first; and where a wall is curved enough to make the offsets genuinely cross, its facets fold before they get there (the `TrimmedFaceRefusalTests` pattern: the verdict is locked by test so the finding cannot rot).
